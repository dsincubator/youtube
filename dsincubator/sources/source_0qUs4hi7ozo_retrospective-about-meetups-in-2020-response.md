# Retrospective about meetups in 2020: Response

## Summary

This session responds to an internal retrospective run by 2 Degrees Investing on the DS Incubator meetups, addressing feedback on what went well, what did not, and how to improve, plus perceptions of impact on everyday work. The speaker frames the meetup as acknowledgement of the effort of giving feedback and as material useful for others running similar community learning projects outside 2 Degrees.

Decisions taken after the retrospective are articulated: nominate a timekeeper each meetup, introduce a closing round of voices where everyone is offered but not required to speak, accept both standalone meetups and series (syllabus) where the latter enables more complex topics and faster preparation, deprecate the must have / nice to have labels in favor of the practices project led by Monica, shift format toward demos rather than live interactive coding, explicitly relate content to 2 Degrees work and to a broader public YouTube audience, and point each meetup issue to resources for follow-up learning including a book summary on Crucial Conversations.

Trade-offs are discussed for schedule (keeping the existing day/time despite back-to-back DS Incubator and Data Herald slots, relying on a timekeeper to avoid disruption), for depth versus breadth (research favors learning by doing, but demos expose participants to more topics while the coding helpdesk and linked resources support deeper individual follow-up), and for cost versus responsiveness (longer interactive workshops give deeper learning but are slow to prepare and reduce the ability to respond quickly to emerging needs, so workshops are viewed as a complement rather than a replacement). Relevance is hard because simple teaching examples hide real-world complexity, so at minimum the link to daily work will be articulated verbally. The closing synthesis is that retrospectives are highly useful and could become a checklist for designing and running meetups, and that the incubator's effect is compound alongside other parallel initiatives, visible when comparing current practices to one to one-and-a-half years earlier.

## Key Concepts & Tools Taught

- **[retrospective]:** Structured review asking what went well, what did not go so well, and what can be done to improve, plus impact, used to shape the incubator's direction for the year.
- **[consulting]:** Decision-making system adopted for the incubator — the lead consults the community for feedback and then makes the decision — contrasted with consensus (used in the practices project where broad buy-in is required), voting, and command, as discussed via Crucial Conversations.
- **[facilitation-checklist]:** Checklist for designing and running each meetup (derived from an earlier, outdated checklist) covering nominating a timekeeper, articulating the audience, relating content to everyday life, pointing to resources, and running the closing round.
- **[round-of-voices]:** Closing facilitation technique giving every participant an opportunity to speak for about a minute (with the option to pass), used to ensure ideas flow and dialogue is captured.
- **[demo-vs-workshop]:** Trade-off between short demo-format meetups (broad exposure, fast to prepare, responsive to emerging needs) and longer interactive workshops/hands-on tutorials (deeper learning by doing but costly to prepare and slower to respond); the plan retains demos and treats workshops as a future complement, including a possible in-person workshop.
- **[series-of-meetups]:** Syllabus-style sequence of two to three or more linked meetups for complex themes — examples proposed include interactivity from zero to hero, targets for data pipelines, package development, and git and GitHub — as opposed to single standalone topics.
